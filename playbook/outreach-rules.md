# Outreach rules

## Principles
- **Lead with them, then with proof, then the ask.** Something specific about their team/product → one line of
  real evidence you can help → a small, easy ask.
- **Small asks win.** "Would you be open to referring me?" after they've replied beats asking cold.
  "Could I ask you two questions about the team?" beats "Can we hop on a 30-min call?"
- **No flattery, no "I hope this finds you well", no "I'm passionate about".**
- **Every claim about the candidate is true** (experience library).
- Indian context: referrals are the dominant channel at product companies; employees often have referral
  bonuses, so a clear, low-effort referral ask (role link + job ID + resume) is welcome, not rude.

## Lengths
| Message | Limit |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn connection note | **≤ 300 characters** (hard limit 300; aim for 200–280) |
| LinkedIn DM / InMail | ≤ 600 characters |
| Cold email | ≤ 120 words, subject ≤ 7 words |
| Follow-up | ≤ 60 words, adds something new (a project link, a work product, a relevant thought) |

## Referral sequence (3 messages)
1. **Ask** (after connection accepted): context on why this team + 1 proof line + "Would you be comfortable referring me for <role, job ID>? Happy to send anything that makes it easy."
2. **Strong push** (4–5 days, no reply): new value (work product, relevant project), make it effortless (resume + JD link + 2-line blurb they can paste).
3. **Hiring-manager identification** (4–5 days later, or if they can't refer): "No worries at all. Do you know who the hiring manager for <team> is? I'd love to share a short analysis I wrote on <product>."

## Follow-up cadence
Connection request → if accepted, ask within 24 h → nudge at day 4 → final at day 9 → mark cold.
Applied without referral → hiring-manager message within 48 h.
After interview → thank-you within 24 h.
